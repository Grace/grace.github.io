Demos

Things easier to show than to describe. Three of these are the real Go tool compiled to WebAssembly and running in your browser — not a reimplementation of it. The ranger demo is different: it is a report the Go tool generated, with the incident data embedded. Nothing is uploaded.

  • ranger — find the service that actually broke root-cause localization
    A real incident from the OpenTelemetry Demo, localized to one service out of 87 ranked operations. Duration says every service on the request path is guilty; self time says which one did the work. This one is a report ranger wrote, not the tool running live — every trace is embedded in the page, and nothing is fetched.
  • paladin — see what your agent reads integrity monitoring
    Paste a CLAUDE.md, .cursorrules or any instruction file and watch the characters your font refuses to draw appear: zero-width spaces, bidi overrides that reorder text on screen, and Cyrillic letters wearing Latin faces. It runs paladin itself, compiled to WebAssembly — not a reimplementation.
  • warden — what a decline is allowed to say rules engines
    A rules engine answers in its own vocabulary — rule ids, working-memory facts, the margin floor that priced the decision. Switch between a public, partner and internal caller and watch what crosses the boundary change, with everything held back listed underneath. It runs warden itself, compiled to WebAssembly.
  • genai-interlingua — normalize a GenAI span OpenTelemetry · three pages
    Six instrumentation libraries spell the same token count six different ways, so nothing about your model calls can be queried as one thing. Paste a span — or pick a real capture — and see it rewritten into one gen_ai.* schema with what the translation cost recorded on the result. Then two pages that go further: the inspector takes one attribute at a time and says which source key produced it, what evidence the reading turned on, and what it could not carry; and what translation costs draws the measurements across all six libraries — the scoring race that picks a dialect, and where 105 attributes with no home in the conventions end up. How it works, in pseudocode.
